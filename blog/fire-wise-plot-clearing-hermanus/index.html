Fire-Wise Plot Clearing in Hermanus: Rules, Costs & Checklist

A practical guide to reducing fire hazards on vacant land without losing sight of protected vegetation, responsible disposal and the unique needs of your property.

By Nick Tree Felling23 November 20269 min read

If you own a vacant plot in Hermanus, fire-wise clearing is about more than making the land look tidy. It means reducing combustible vegetation and other fire hazards while following municipal requirements and protecting the natural features that should remain.

The Overstrand Municipality has a specific policy for creating and maintaining fire-wise vacant erven. This guide turns its main homeowner considerations into practical steps. It does not replace the policy or an instruction from the municipality, but it will help you prepare for a clearing quote and ask the right questions.

The short answer

Owners must maintain vacant erven so they do not create a fire hazard. That does not mean stripping every property bare. Clearing should account for ground cover, trees, invasive vegetation, nearby structures, soil erosion and protected species.

What does fire-wise plot clearing mean?

Fire-wise clearing reduces the material and conditions that could allow a vegetation fire to start or spread. On a typical property, that may involve cutting back excessive growth, removing deadwood, addressing invasive plants, lifting vegetation beneath tree canopies and disposing of the resulting refuse.

The goal is a managed property, not necessarily a bare one. Indigenous ground cover can help protect topsoil against erosion and drought, while established trees may need trimming rather than removal. A responsible clearing plan distinguishes between a hazard and vegetation that can safely remain.

Does the Overstrand policy apply to your plot?

The policy approved by the Overstrand Municipality on 28 September 2022 applies specifically to municipal and privately owned vacant land within residential areas and bordering urban edges. It addresses fire hazards presented by overgrown erven and sets out roles for landowners and the municipality.

Landowners are expected to maintain their vacant erven in a satisfactory condition, as determined by the Chief Fire Officer or the relevant delegated authority. If a property is identified as a hazard, the municipality can begin a compliance process.

  • Do not assume “vacant” means “no maintenance needed.” An unused stand can still require active vegetation management.
  • Do not wait for a notice if the hazard is obvious. Early maintenance is usually easier to plan than urgent clearing.
  • Check the current rules. Policies can be reviewed or amended, and other environmental or planning requirements may also apply.

Your fire-wise plot-clearing checklist

The exact work required depends on the erf. Use this checklist as a conversation starter during an assessment, not as permission to carry out every item without checking the site.

1. Identify structures, neighbours and access routes

Record nearby homes, boundary walls, thatched roofs, roads and any way workers or vehicles can enter. Note slopes, restricted access and areas where soil disturbance could cause erosion. These details influence both the method and the quote.

2. Remove deadwood and combustible waste

The policy calls for erven to be cleared of deadwood and combustible material not associated with growing vegetation. Dumped rubble, refuse and old timber should not be confused with ordinary garden waste; different disposal arrangements may be necessary.

3. Address invasive alien vegetation

Invasive alien vegetation must be dealt with in accordance with applicable legislation and the policy. Simply cutting a plant once may not prevent regrowth. Ask about the appropriate treatment and follow-up for the species on your land.

4. Manage grass and ground cover without exposing soil

The policy sets a maximum height of 500 mm for grass and indigenous ground-covering plant species, while recognising the protective role of ground cover. Clearing methods should avoid unnecessary soil disturbance and erosion.

5. Reduce growth beneath tree canopies

The area around trees may require growth to be cleared to create space beneath the canopy, together with removal of ground-level deadwood. This is different from topping or indiscriminately removing the tree.

6. Give thatched structures special attention

Erven within 50 metres of thatched-roof structures receive particular consideration in the policy. Trees are handled separately from the vegetation-clearing standard, so a close-site assessment is essential.

7. Arrange refuse removal before work starts

Vegetation refuse may not be left on an erf or verge for more than three days under the 2022 policy. Chipping is addressed separately: chips must be handled in the permitted manner and not left in heaps that could create a combustion risk.

A good clearing quote explains not only what will be cut, but also what will remain, how waste will be handled and how the property will be left.

Protected trees and vegetation: check before you cut

Fire-wise clearing does not override tree-protection laws. The Overstrand policy specifically discusses white milkwood and other protected or endangered plants. It notes restrictions on pruning and prohibits topping.

For milkwood, the policy refers to a maximum of 25% pruning under the applicable exemption, with important exclusions for natural forest and other protected contexts. Destroying protected trees, natural forest or protected plants without the necessary permit can be a criminal offence.

If you are unsure what is growing on your plot, pause before cutting. Read our guide to tree-removal permission in Hermanus and confirm the requirements with the appropriate authority.

Two plots of the same size can require very different amounts of work. A meaningful quote should reflect the actual site rather than a generic price per square metre.

Received a municipal compliance notice?

Read the notice carefully and note the property description, required work and deadline. The 2022 policy refers to a 28-working-day reducing compliance period for owners residing in South Africa and 40 working days for owners residing outside the country. Do not rely on the general article alone—use the deadline and instructions on your actual notice.

Contact the municipality if anything is unclear, particularly where protected vegetation or access is involved. Then provide the notice to your clearing contractor so the scope can be discussed accurately.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to clear my vacant erf in the Overstrand?

You must maintain it so it does not present a fire hazard. The municipality determines whether the property meets its standards. Follow the applicable policy rather than removing all vegetation indiscriminately.

Can I remove every tree when clearing a plot?

No. Protected trees and plants require special care and may require authorisation. Identify trees before clearing, and do not treat fire-wise clearing as permission to remove protected species.

How long can clearing refuse remain on my erf?

The 2022 policy states that vegetation refuse may not remain on an erf or verge for more than three days. Chipped material must be handled as the policy permits.

How much does plot clearing cost in Hermanus?
