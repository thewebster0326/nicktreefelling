How to Prepare Your Property for Tree Felling: A Homeowner’s Checklist

A few practical steps before the team arrives can make tree work safer, faster and easier to manage. Use this checklist to prepare without putting yourself at risk.

By Nick Tree Felling23 November 20267 min read

Before tree felling, clear small movable items from the work and access areas, keep people and pets away, tell the contractor about obstacles or underground services, and agree on exactly what will happen to the stump and debris. Do not cut branches or move heavy objects into the work zone yourself.

Good preparation starts with information. A contractor who knows about your access, neighbours and priorities can plan a safer, more efficient job. Use this checklist after the work is booked and confirm anything you are unsure about before the team arrives.

1. Confirm the scope before the appointment

Make sure everyone is discussing the same tree and the same finish. A simple “remove the tree” can mean very different things to a homeowner and a contractor.

  • Which tree or trees are included?
  • Is the work full removal, trimming or sectional dismantling?
  • Will the stump be left, ground or extracted?
  • Will branches and trunk sections be stacked, left for your use or removed?
  • Is loading, transport, disposal and final cleanup included?
  • Are there access, travel or other additional considerations?

If the quote is unclear, ask before work starts. Our Hermanus tree-felling price guide includes a scope checklist you can use for comparison.

2. Share useful photographs and information

Photographs help the team prepare, especially when an on-site assessment has not yet taken place. Include the entire tree from base to crown, its base, nearby structures and the route workers will use.

Mention anything that may not be obvious: a weak wall, new paving, irrigation, underground cables, a septic system, delicate plants or a neighbour’s access requirement. If you know a branch is dead, cracked or hanging, point it out from a safe position rather than inspecting it closely.

3. Clear the access route

Workers may need to carry ropes, tools and heavy sections between the tree and a vehicle. Remove small movable obstacles along that route.

  • Garden furniture, toys, pots, ornaments and lightweight decorations
  • Hoses, irrigation fittings and loose garden tools
  • Bicycles, bins, trailers and movable storage items
  • Vehicles from the driveway or loading area
  • Laundry, pool covers and items that could be damaged by dust or debris

Do not attempt to move heavy structures, prune branches or create a fall zone by cutting vegetation yourself. If something is too large or risky to move, leave it and tell the team.

4. Plan for children, pets and other people

Arrange for children and pets to remain indoors or away from the property’s work area. A gate that normally stays open may need to be closed for safety while still allowing agreed worker access.

Let tenants, staff, visitors or other regular users know that work is taking place. The contractor should establish and manage the actual exclusion zone; your role is to help make sure other people do not wander into it.

5. Speak to neighbours where appropriate

If work may affect shared access, create noise near a boundary or require coordination around overhanging branches, notify the relevant neighbour in advance. This is particularly important when the tree’s ownership or the pruning scope has been discussed previously.

Do not give a contractor permission to enter someone else’s property unless you are authorised to do so. For unresolved ownership or branch disputes, read our guide to neighbour’s trees in South Africa and get the agreement or advice you need first.

6. Identify utilities and property features

Overhead cables change the risk immediately. Keep your distance and tell the contractor before work begins. Additional coordination with the electricity authority may be needed; do not assume a tree crew can work around energised lines as though they were ordinary obstacles.

For stump work or excavation, discuss known underground services and irrigation. Mark known features if you can do so safely, but do not rely on memory alone where plans or professional locating may be required.

7. Check permissions and protected-tree issues

Private ownership does not automatically mean every tree can be removed. Protected species, municipal verges, estate rules and development conditions can change the requirements.

Make sure applications, approvals and property documents have been considered before the appointment. Our Hermanus tree-permission guide explains the main checks, with official sources.

8. Be flexible about unsafe conditions

High wind, heavy rain, lightning or other unsafe conditions may require work to be rescheduled. A responsible contractor should not proceed simply because a date was agreed.

If the tree is immediately dangerous, keep people away and call for guidance. Do not climb onto a roof or attempt emergency cutting yourself. Our dangerous-tree warning guide explains which changes need urgent attention.

9. On the day of the job

  1. Unlock agreed gates or arrange access with the team.
  2. Keep vehicles away from the loading and work areas.
  3. Point out any new obstacles or changes since the quotation.
  4. Confirm the tree, stump and cleanup scope one last time.
  5. Stay outside the contractor’s exclusion zone and follow site instructions.

Afterwards, walk through the agreed finish with the team where safe to do so. Confirm that the stump, timber, refuse and cleanup match the quote before the site is considered complete.

Printable homeowner checklist

  • Tree and service confirmed
  • Stump decision made
  • Refuse and cleanup agreed
  • Photos and access details shared
  • Small movable items cleared
  • Vehicles moved
  • Children and pets arranged
  • Neighbours informed where needed
  • Utilities and irrigation identified
  • Permissions checked
  • Weather concerns discussed
  • Final walkthrough completed

Frequently asked questions

Should I cut branches before the tree team arrives?

No. Do not create additional cutting work or hazards before the assessment. Clear only small movable items and leave tree work to the planned team.

Do I need to be home during tree felling?

Discuss this with the contractor. Someone authorised should normally provide access and be contactable for scope or site questions, but you should stay outside the exclusion zone.

Should I inform my neighbours?

Yes where noise, shared access, boundary branches or potential disruption may affect them. Shared property access must be agreed with the relevant owner or occupier.

What should I move before tree removal?

Move vehicles, garden furniture, toys, hoses, pots, ornaments and other small items from the work and access areas. Leave heavy or risky objects for discussion with the team.

What if the weather is bad on the appointment day?

The work may need to change or be rescheduled. High wind, rain and lightning can make tree work unsafe, so follow the contractor’s safety assessment.
