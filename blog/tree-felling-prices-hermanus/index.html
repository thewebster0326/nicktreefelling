Tree Felling Prices in Hermanus: What Affects Your Quote?

A clear look at published South African ranges, the work behind the price, and how to compare tree-felling quotes without missing important costs.

By Nick Tree Felling23 November 20268 min read

There is no single reliable average price for tree felling in Hermanus. Published South African guides show broad ranges from about R800 for some small jobs to R15,000 or more for very large or complex removals. Those figures can help with early budgeting, but they cannot tell you what your particular tree will cost.

A fair quote reflects the height and spread of the tree, where it stands, how it must be dismantled, and what happens to the timber and branches afterwards. Two trees of the same species and height can have very different removal costs.

Tree size is only the starting point

Height influences how much material must be handled and how workers reach the crown, but trunk diameter, crown spread and branch structure matter too. A wide, heavily branched tree can produce much more timber and require more cuts than a narrow tree of the same height.

Condition also matters. Dead, cracked or decayed wood may behave unpredictably and require a different approach from a healthy tree. The safest method is determined by the site, not by a price table.

Access and the working area

An open stand with room to fell a tree in one controlled direction is very different from a small enclosed garden. Consider everything between the tree and the point where branches or logs can be loaded.

  • Can workers and equipment reach the tree easily?
  • Are there steps, narrow gates, steep paths or soft ground?
  • How far must branches and trunk sections be carried?
  • Is there space to establish a safe exclusion zone?
  • Can a vehicle get close enough for efficient loading?

Restricted access does not make every job expensive, but it changes labour and equipment requirements and should be visible in the quote.

Nearby buildings, cables and other risks

Where a tree stands over a roof, glass structure, boundary wall, pool, vehicle or overhead service, contractors may need to dismantle it in sections and lower those sections with ropes. This is usually more time-consuming than open felling.

Work near energised power lines requires particular caution and may need coordination with the relevant electricity authority. A tree contractor should not treat an overhead cable as just another branch obstacle.

Tell the contractor about anything that might not be obvious in a photograph, including underground services, irrigation, delicate paving and restricted neighbours’ access.

What should the quote include?

The biggest pricing misunderstandings happen when two quotes have different scopes. Ask for the work to be described in enough detail for you to compare like with like.

Scope itemWhat to clarify
Felling or dismantlingWill the tree be felled whole or removed in controlled sections?
Branch and trunk handlingWill timber be cut into manageable sections, stacked or left in place?
Stump workIs the stump being left, ground, extracted or treated where appropriate?
Refuse removalAre loading, transport and disposal included?
Final cleanupDoes the price include raking, sweeping and removal of small debris?
Additional travelIs travel to your town accounted for in the quote?

A lower quote that leaves a large stump and all the branches behind may not be the same service as a higher quote that includes complete removal and cleanup.

How different job scenarios affect pricing

Small tree in an open garden

Access is straightforward and there is room to work. The main remaining questions are disposal and whether the stump must be removed.

Large tree beside a house

Sectional dismantling, rigging and protection of the building become central. Crown spread and the size of the safe working zone can matter more than a simple height measurement.

Dead tree near a boundary

Condition can affect the choice of climbing and cutting method. Ownership, neighbouring property and protected-species checks may also be relevant.

Several trees on one property

Multiple trees can make setup and travel more efficient, but the total work and disposal volume still increase. Ask for the quote to identify each tree clearly.

Tree work as part of plot clearing

Established trees may need to be separated from bush clearing and priced as a distinct task. Read our Hermanus plot-clearing guide for the related cost factors.

A fair way to compare tree-felling quotes

  1. Make sure each quote identifies the same tree or trees. Photographs and simple labels can prevent confusion.
  2. Compare methods, not just totals. One contractor may have allowed for controlled sectional removal while another has not described it.
  3. Check stump and debris inclusions. These can account for a substantial part of the difference.
  4. Discuss protection of the property. Ask how roofs, walls, paving and planting will be considered.
  5. Confirm timing and any additional charges. Urgent scheduling, travel and difficult disposal should be discussed before acceptance.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average tree-felling price in Hermanus?

There is no reliable single average. Published South African guides show broad ranges from about R800 for some small jobs to R15,000 or more for very large or complex removals. Your actual quote depends on the tree and site.

Does tree-felling include removing the stump?

Not always. Felling usually refers to bringing down the tree, while stump grinding or extraction is a separate task on many quotes. Confirm before comparing prices.

Is debris removal included?

Inclusions vary. Ask whether branches, trunk sections, loading, transport, disposal and final cleanup are covered.

Why does a tree near a house cost more to remove?

Nearby structures often require climbing, sectional dismantling and controlled lowering rather than felling the tree in one piece. This can add labour, equipment and time.

Can I get a quote from photographs?

Clear photographs can support an initial estimate when they show the whole tree, base, access and nearby structures. Complex or high-risk jobs may still require a site visit.
